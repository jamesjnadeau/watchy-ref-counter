# What has not been checked

`checks/run.sh` proves the netlist matches the spec. That is the only thing
that has been proved. Everything below is open, and every item is blocking
before this board is sent to a fab house.

- **The RV-3028-C7 footprint is provisional.** `elec/footprints/footprints.pretty/RV-3028-C7.kicad_mod` was drawn from the C7 package outline (3.20 x 1.50 x 0.80mm, 8 terminals, 4 per long side) with a reconstructed land pattern: 0.90mm pitch, 0.50 x 0.50mm pads, rows 1.20mm centre to centre. Those numbers were NOT read off the datasheet's recommended-solder-pad drawing -- network egress to microcrystal.com and every datasheet mirror is blocked in the environment this was built in. Get the datasheet, correct the footprint, then print it at 1:1 and lay the real part on it. A footprint wrong by half a pitch is invisible on screen and obvious on paper.
- **The RV-3028-C7 pin numbering is provisional for the same reason.** The pin functions (1 CLKOUT, 2 INT, 3 SCL, 4 SDA, 5 VSS, 6 VBACKUP, 7 VDD, 8 EVI) come from the spec; which physical corner is pin 1, and whether numbering runs counter-clockwise, was assumed when drawing the footprint. Confirm both against the datasheet.
- **No ERC has been run, and none can be.** atopile has no ERC, and `kicad-cli sch erc` reads a `.kicad_sch`, which this project does not have -- the design is atopile source compiled straight to a netlist. Nothing has checked for driven-output conflicts or unconnected pins beyond what the netlist assertions cover. This is a real cost of the "design is code" choice and it is not going away.
- **DRC now runs, and `checks/run.sh` fails on it.** KiCad 9.0.2 is installed and `checks/drc_check.py` holds the board to zero errors and zero unconnected items against a written allowlist. What DRC does *not* tell you is whether the design is right -- only that the copper obeys the rules.
- **Three signal nets are not routed:** `EPD_C24` at `J1.24`, `EPD_CS` at `J1.12` and `USB_DM` at `J2.A7`. Every ground pad is connected as of the current run, but stranded ground pads -- reached by no pour and given no via -- are the failure this layout produces when the right-hand side gets crowded, and earlier runs stranded `U3.2` and `C11.1`, an ESD array's return and a bulk capacitor's ground. They were invisible until the floating pour slivers concealing them were deleted, so treat a run that reports none as a result of that run rather than a property of the design. `scripts/route.py` finishes the power and all but three of the signal nets and leaves the rest; it exits non-zero and `checks/run.sh` fails while they are outstanding. The board is not finished and nothing in this repository should be read as saying it is. The README lists what was tried and what to try next.
- **DRC has no unaccepted errors left, which is not the same as the board being right.** 39 violations remain and every one is on `checks/drc_check.py`'s allowlist with a written reason: `U1`'s 17 pads 0.30mm from the top edge (the module's position is fixed by its antenna having to overhang), 18 courtyard overlaps, and `J2`'s own mounting holes against its own pads. An allowlist is a record of decisions, not a proof that the decisions were good -- read it before trusting it. What DRC still reports as outstanding is connectivity: 4 unconnected items, from the three unrouted nets and one stranded ground pad. Superseded figures, for reference: 128 errors: 63 solder-mask bridges, 21 board-edge-clearance, 21 starved thermals, 18 courtyard overlaps, 4 hole-clearance, 1 clearance. Every mask bridge is now between pads of one fine-pitch footprint (`J1` 22, `J2` 19, `U3` 4) or between two adjacent 0402s, which is ordinary aperture merging and what a fab expects; every edge-clearance hit is a pad rather than a track; and the clearance and hole-clearance errors are internal to `Q4`/`R15` and to `J2`. What is *not* yet done is writing each of those into `checks/drc_check.py`'s allowlist with a reason, so the suite still fails on all 128 rather than on the ones that matter.
- **V2's branding has been taken off the board.** The `watchy` and `SQFMI` mask lettering (19 text objects) and the two sqfmi logo footprints are deleted by `apply_netlist.py` on every run. This is a derivative reference design and should not be fabricated carrying another project's marks; it also removed 119 solder-mask bridges, because mask lettering is drawn as bare-copper apertures and everything passing beneath one bridged to it. The display outline (`REF**`, B.Fab) is kept -- it comes from V2's library and carries the name, but it is a mechanical outline rather than a mark. Attribution to sqfmi stays in the documentation, where it belongs.
- **The routing is machine-generated and has never been reviewed by eye.** `scripts/route.py` is a maze router written for this board. It gets the connectivity right, DRC agrees, and the differential pair and charge-pump loop are routed first and never ripped up -- but "DRC-clean" is a very long way from "well routed". Nobody has looked at the return paths, the loop areas, or what the USB pair actually does between the connector and the module. Do that before ordering.
- **The USB pair is not impedance-controlled.** `USB_DP` and `USB_DM` are routed first, together, on F.Cu over the In1.Cu ground plane, at the board's default 0.2mm width. Nothing has computed what that geometry gives for a 90-ohm differential pair against the stackup, and the stackup itself is nominal.
- **Three courtyard overlaps remain and are accepted on purpose**, all of them the USB-C receptacle against its neighbours: `J2`/`R16` by 0.49mm, `J2`/`J3` by 0.33mm and `J2`/`Q4` by 0.13mm. There is 9.15mm between the battery connector and the charger, and a USB-C shell is 9.04mm wide with a 10.73mm courtyard, so this does not fit and cannot be made to fit without moving a part the case is cut to. The parts themselves clear each other; it is the courtyards that overlap. Everything else V2 carried has been left as V2 had it.
- **Three parts moved to get copper off the USB-C shield tabs, and one of them faces the case.** The tabs are through-hole and stick out past the housing; `J3`'s mounting pad and `U5.3` were sitting *on* one, overlapping by 0.067mm and 0.120mm, which is a short and not a clearance. `U5` moved down 0.45mm, `J3` up 0.28mm, and `SW2` -- the BACK button -- up 0.20mm, because `J3` is pinched between the tab and the button and the corridor was 0.05mm too narrow to fit it otherwise. The button move is inside any plausible case tolerance but it is a real change to a case-facing part; the case spec records it. Two V2-inherited pad pairs are still under the 0.2mm rule (`Q4.3`/`R15.1` at 0.165mm, `R14.2`/`Q4.1` at 0.175mm) and have been left as V2 had them.
- **`VBUS` is a pour, and the split under it has not been thought about hard.** It sits on In2.Cu at higher priority than `+3V3`, over the nine pads of the charge-current block; `D4` and `D5` are still routed. That puts a hole in the `+3V3` plane, and anything routed on B.Cu across that hole has no continuous reference underneath it. At SPI rates and 12Mbps USB this should not matter, and the pair itself is on F.Cu over solid In1.Cu ground -- but nobody has looked at which B.Cu tracks actually cross the split.
- **The `VBACKUP` decision is reasoned, not measured.** Tying `VBACKUP` to `GND` rather than to a rail is argued from the direct-switchover behaviour described in the RV-3028-C7 application manual. Read that section before committing to it.
- **The USB-C receptacle is a footprint choice, not a sourcing decision.** `Connector_USB:USB_C_Receptacle_XKB_U262-16XN-4BVC11` is a common 16-pin part. Its *pose* has now been checked -- it is rotated 90 degrees, and its housing front face is flush with the board edge at x=102.5, which puts all sixteen pads and both shield tabs on the board. It was previously at 0 degrees, facing down the board, with six pads hanging over the right-hand edge in mid-air. The case cutout has still not been checked against it.
- **The board has no strap slots any more.** V2 cut two 2 x 28mm slots through the PCB for the band to pin through. They ran directly under the module and its decoupling and have been removed. Nothing on the PCB now takes the strap load -- the case has to, in full. See `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-18-waterproof-case-design.md`.
- **The module's thermal pads are tied to the ground plane with vias in the pads.** They have to be: the array is 1.2mm pads on a 1.65mm pitch, which leaves 0.45mm gaps that a pour cannot enter and a via cannot sit beside. Espressif's reference layout does the same. Tell the fab, and expect to plug or tent them -- an open via in an SMD pad wicks solder off it.
- **The firmware's pin map for this board was wrong in three places, and is now right by inspection only.** `RefCounter/board.h`'s ESP32-S3 block had the four button pins as 7/6/0/8 where that board wired them to IO4/5/6/7 -- every line wrong, and `PIN_BTN_UP 0` is the boot strapping pin, which this design uses as `TP_BOOT`. It also carried `BATT_DIVIDER` as `(360+100)/360` = 1.28 where this board's 1M/1M divider needs 2.0, so the battery would have read about 36% low. Both are corrected against `elec/src/watchy.ato` and the spec's pin map, and all five PlatformIO environments build -- but nothing has run on hardware, and a pin map that agrees with itself is not the same as one that agrees with a board. `RTC_INT` (IO4 on the C6) still has no firmware constant at all; whether that is a gap depends on whether the RTC alarm is meant to be a wake source in this firmware, and nobody has checked.
- **The six display GPIOs are ordered for the layout and do not match Watchy V3.** Stock Watchy firmware will drive the wrong lines on this board -- and since the module is now an ESP32-C6, there are no IO33-IO48 for it to match anyway. This was a deliberate choice and stock-firmware compatibility is explicitly not a goal; `RefCounter/board.h` is the authoritative map. See the spec's pin map section.
- **The GDEY0154D67 pinout is assumed to match the GDEH0154D67 it replaces.** The GDEH is end-of-life. Both are 1.54" 200x200 on an SSD1681 with a 24-pin FPC, GxEPD2 drives both with the same four-pin constructor, and the firmware builds -- but the panel's FPC pinout has not been read off a GDEY datasheet and compared pin by pin against what `J1` is wired to. Do that before powering a panel.
- **Nothing about the ESP32-C6-MINI-1 swap has been checked against hardware.** The pin map is derived from the datasheet's Table 3-1, Table 4-3 and Table 4-7 and from ESP-IDF's C6 GPIO and sleep-mode documentation: 22 GPIO brought out, IO0-IO7 the only deep-sleep wake pins, IO0-IO6 the only ADC1 channels, IO9 the only pin that decides boot mode, IO15 ignored unless an eFuse is burnt. `checks/netlist_check.py` asserts the design agrees with all of that, and mutation testing confirms the assertions bite -- but every one of those facts is a reading of a PDF, and the board has never been powered. The `watchy_c6` firmware env also builds on a *different* PlatformIO platform from the other four (the pioarduino fork, for Arduino-ESP32 3.x); that toolchain has never produced a binary that ran on anything.

- **The ESP32-C6-MINI-1 land pattern is Espressif's own** (github.com/espressif/kicad-libraries, CC-BY-SA 4.0), which is about as good as a sourced footprint gets, but it has still never been through a reflow oven on this board.
- **Nothing here has been fabricated, powered or tested.** No claim in this repository should be read as saying the design works.
